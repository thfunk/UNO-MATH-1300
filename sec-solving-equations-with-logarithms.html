    Section 5.4 Solving Equations with Logarithms

    Logarithmic and exponential functions are inverses. This inverse relationship allows us to solve equations in which the variable appears in an exponent or inside a logarithm. Solutions may be written exactly with logarithms or approximated with a calculator.
    🔗

    Objectives: Learning Objectives

    1. Solve exponential equations with base \(e\) by applying the natural logarithm.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Solve exponential equations with other bases by using logarithms.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Distinguish between exact and approximate solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Solve logarithmic equations by applying the corresponding exponential function.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Check restrictions and reject invalid logarithmic solutions.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.4.1 Logarithms Undo Exponential Functions

    If \(g(x)=b^x\text{,}\) then its inverse is \(g^{-1}(x)=\log_b(x)\text{.}\) Therefore, the equation \(b^x=A\) can be solved by applying a logarithm:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} x=\log_b(A)=\frac{\ln(A)}{\ln(b)}. \end{equation*}

    Example 5.4.1. Exact and Approximate Forms.

    Solve \(2^x=70\text{.}\) Give an exact solution and an approximation to three decimal places.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Apply the natural logarithm to both sides:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \ln(2^x)&=\ln(70),\\ x\ln(2)&=\ln(70),\\ x&=\frac{\ln(70)}{\ln(2)}. \end{align*}
    The exact solution is \(\frac{\ln(70)}{\ln(2)}\text{.}\) Using a calculator, \(x\approx6.129\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Remark 5.4.2.

    An exact answer retains logarithms. An approximate answer replaces the logarithmic expression with a rounded decimal.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.4.2 Equations with Base e

    1. Isolate the exponential expression.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Apply \(\ln\) to both sides.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Use \(\ln(e^u)=u\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Solve the resulting equation.
      🔗
      🔗

    Example 5.4.3. A Natural Exponential Equation.

    Solve \(e^{-0.35t}=\frac{1}{4}\text{.}\) Give an exact solution and an approximation.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \ln\left(e^{-0.35t}\right)&=\ln\left(\frac14\right),\\ -0.35t&=\ln\left(\frac14\right),\\ t&=\frac{\ln(1/4)}{-0.35}. \end{align*}
    Thus, \(t\approx3.961\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.4.4. Isolate the Exponential Expression First.

    Solve \(18=50e^{-0.22t}+6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First isolate the exponential expression:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 12&=50e^{-0.22t},\\ \frac{6}{25}&=e^{-0.22t}. \end{align*}
    Now apply the natural logarithm:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \ln\left(\frac{6}{25}\right)&=-0.22t,\\ t&=\frac{\ln(6/25)}{-0.22}\approx6.488. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.4.3 Exponential Equations with Other Bases

    When the base is not \(e\text{,}\) we can still apply \(\ln\) to both sides. The power property, \(\ln(b^x)=x\ln(b)\text{,}\) brings the variable out of the exponent.
    🔗

    Example 5.4.5. Growth with an Initial Value.

    Solve \(420=75(1.18)^t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Divide by 75 before applying a logarithm:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 5.6&=(1.18)^t,\\ \ln(5.6)&=\ln\left((1.18)^t\right),\\ \ln(5.6)&=t\ln(1.18),\\ t&=\frac{\ln(5.6)}{\ln(1.18)}\approx10.410. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.4.6. An Equation with a Vertical Shift.

    Solve \(36=11(1.09)^{x+2}+3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Isolate the exponential expression:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 33&=11(1.09)^{x+2},\\ 3&=(1.09)^{x+2}. \end{align*}
    Apply \(\ln\) and use the power property:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \ln(3)&=(x+2)\ln(1.09),\\ x+2&=\frac{\ln(3)}{\ln(1.09)},\\ x&=\frac{\ln(3)}{\ln(1.09)}-2\approx10.747. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Warning 5.4.7.

    Do not apply a logarithm until the exponential expression is isolated. For example, in \(36=11(1.09)^{x+2}+3\text{,}\) subtract 3 and divide by 11 before taking logarithms.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.4.4 Equations Containing Logarithms

    To solve an equation containing one logarithm, first isolate the logarithm. Then rewrite the equation in exponential form.
    🔗

    Example 5.4.8. A Common Logarithmic Equation.

    Solve \(2\log(x-4)+1=7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Isolate the logarithm:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 2\log(x-4)&=6,\\ \log(x-4)&=3. \end{align*}
    Rewrite in exponential form:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} x-4&=10^3,\\ x&=1004. \end{align*}
    Because \(x-4>0\text{,}\) the solution is valid.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.4.9. A Natural Logarithmic Equation.

    Solve \(5-3\ln(2x)=-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} -3\ln(2x)&=-6,\\ \ln(2x)&=2,\\ 2x&=e^2,\\ x&=\frac{e^2}{2}. \end{align*}
    The exact solution is \(\frac{e^2}{2}\text{,}\) and \(x\approx3.695\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.4.10. Checking the Logarithm’s Domain.

    Solve \(\ln(x-1)=\ln(5-x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Since the natural logarithm is one-to-one, equal logarithms with the same base have equal arguments:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} x-1&=5-x,\\ 2x&=6,\\ x&=3. \end{align*}
    Checking the arguments gives \(3-1=2>0\) and \(5-3=2>0\text{,}\) so \(x=3\) is valid.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 5.4.5 Choosing a Method

    • Isolate the exponential or logarithmic expression before applying its inverse.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Use the power property to move a variable out of an exponent.
      🔗
      🔗
    • State exact answers with logarithms before rounding.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Check that every logarithm has a positive argument.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Logarithms and exponentials undo one another. To solve an exponential equation, isolate the exponential expression and apply a logarithm. To solve a logarithmic equation, isolate the logarithm and rewrite in exponential form. Exact logarithmic expressions preserve the full solution, while decimal approximations provide practical numerical values.
